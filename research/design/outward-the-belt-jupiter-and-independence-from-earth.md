# Intercept Window: Outward – The Belt, Jupiter and Independence from Earth

Nov 5, 2026 · proposal · continues [`design/early-economy-revenue-streams-and-the-growth-loop.md`](early-economy-revenue-streams-and-the-growth-loop.md) and [`design/from-contracts-to-markets-starting-actors-and-the-transition.md`](from-contracts-to-markets-starting-actors-and-the-transition.md)

What pulls an economy that started at the Moon out to the belt, what pulls it on to Jupiter, and what a place has to be able to do before it no longer needs Earth. Figures marked *(calc)* were computed for this doc; resource figures come from [`studies/solar-resources/`](../studies/solar-resources/) unless another source is given.

## The short version

- **No new system is needed to move the frontier.** The landed-cost price field, the yield curves and the drive blocks already make rings of profitable activity around each market, as in von Thünen's model of farms around a city. Every drive generation and every depleted rich tail moves the rings outward.
- **The Moon's ice is not what runs out first; its chemistry is.** At 100,000 t of water a year (the lunar yard example) the baseline 1 Gt lasts 10,000 years *(calc)*. What the Moon really lacks is **carbon and nitrogen** (about 100 ppm each) for a growing population's food and air, and **cheap metal**, since lunar metal has to be reduced from oxide in a 2.4 km/s escape well.
- **So people pull the economy to the belt.** Every person in space needs the CHNOPS bundle (carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, sulfur). C-type asteroids and Ceres have it: water, ammonia, carbon and phosphates. Every new yard and habitat needs structure, and metal asteroids have native iron-nickel that needs no reduction.
- **Other pulls and pushes to the belt:** Mars as a market (Mars is closer to the belt than Earth is in Δv), crowding and law at the lunar poles, distance from Earth's reach, and room for big shipyards. Near-Earth asteroids are the stepping stone: thousands are easier to return from than the lunar surface.
- **Jupiter is not needed for its resources while the belt is young.** Ceres alone holds about 230 million times the lunar baseline's water *(calc)*. Jupiter's pulls are different: **prestige science** (Europa's ocean starts a new contract era), **the gateway to Saturn** (Titan's nitrogen and carbon, ice-giant helium-3), **the Trojans** (open real estate outside the radiation belts), **fusion-era fuel**, and **strategic depth**. Callisto is the base: about 0.1 mSv a day at the surface, low enough to live with.
- **Late in the game the main driver is people choosing where to live.** Once a place can support itself and the cost of living there has fallen, people move for room, law, ideology, family and wages. The migration model already reads wages, burden and risk; it needs affinities as well.
- **Independence is a capability ladder:** survive (life support closed) → maintain (replace what wears out) → grow (build new plant, fabs included) → innovate (own research) → defend (drives, sensors, fuel, warships). The settlement labels of the bases doc already give the population each rung roughly needs.
- **Before fusion, no region closes everything alone.** The inner system has metals and fissiles; the outer system has volatiles. Independence before fusion is a property of a **network**, and the trade lanes between its nodes are what an enemy cuts. **Catalysed D-D fusion, which needs only water, removes the last inward dependence**, so it's the biggest single independence technology in the game.

## One rule moves everything outward: the rent gradient

Von Thünen's 1826 model explains why activities sort into rings around a market: the value of producing something at a place is its market price minus its production cost minus the cost of carrying it there. Cheap-to-move, high-value goods can come from far away; bulky, cheap ones have to be made close.

The game already has every term:

- **Market price** is the landed-cost price field at each node ([`design/early-economy-revenue-streams-and-the-growth-loop.md`](early-economy-revenue-streams-and-the-growth-loop.md)).
- **Production cost** is the yield curve at each deposit ([`design/resource-fields-grades-and-yield-curves.md`](resource-fields-grades-and-yield-curves.md)): it rises as the rich tail is taken.
- **Transport cost** is the four costs of moving a tonne ([`studies/interplanetary-bulk-logistics-mines-depots-and-shipyards.md`](../studies/interplanetary-bulk-logistics-mines-depots-and-shipyards.md)), set by the drive blocks.

Three things move the rings outward, and all three already exist as numbers:

1. **Better drives** cut transport cost per km/s and per year, so a farther source beats a nearer one.
2. **Depletion of the rich tail** raises the near source's marginal cost. Near half a lunar cold trap's water sits in a tenth of its ground, and once that tenth is mined the curve climbs.
3. **Demand that the near source can't meet at all**, because the element isn't there: nitrogen and carbon at the Moon, fissiles in the belt, metals in the outer system.

**The map view:** draw the rings. For each good at each market, colour the solar system by where supply is cheapest. The player watches the belt's ring touch cislunar space when nuclear drives arrive.

## Moon to belt: what pulls and what pushes

| Driver | Why the Moon can't do it | Where it points | When it bites |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CHNOPS for people** | Carbon and nitrogen are about 100 ppm in lunar soil; polar ice carries some volatiles, but not a growing population's farms | C-type NEAs (water, C, N as ammonium, P, salts), then Ceres (water, ammonia, carbon, salts) | As soon as population in space grows past crew rotations; food closure is the first need |
| **Metal for big construction** | Lunar metal must be reduced from oxide (molten regolith electrolysis is about 56 kWh per kg of product) and lifted out of a 2.4 km/s well | M- and S-type NEAs (native Fe-Ni, carbonyl refining), then Psyche-class bodies | When yards and habitats need structure in thousands of tonnes |
| **Water beyond the rich tail** | Lunar ice is 10⁸–6×10⁹ t; its rich tail is small and contested | Hydrated NEAs; Ceres at about 2.3×10²⁰ kg | When propellant demand climbs the yield curve far enough |
| **Mars as a market** | Mars is a separate well and a long way from the Moon in time | The belt: Mars → Ceres is a 2.4 km/s departure against 4.9 from Earth | When Mars settlements grow (the triangle trade) |
| **Crowding and law at the poles** | A few cold traps and lit ridges, held by blocs under two rulebooks | NEAs and the belt, where claims are open and nobody's law has arrived | From the start, for second-tier companies and late entrants |
| **Distance from Earth's reach** | The Moon is three days from Earth's fleets | The belt: windows, months of transit, the host's deliverable force drops | When a company or community wants autonomy |
| **Room for big hulls** | Lunar-orbit yards depend on lunar shuttles and Earth vitamins | Belt yards with native metal and local volatiles | When torch-era hulls grow past what cislunar supply can feed |

**Near-Earth asteroids are the stepping stone.** Several thousand known NEAs are energetically easier to return from than the lunar surface, and the Keck-style retriever needs only about 160 m/s to put its catch into high lunar orbit. Companies learn bagging, carbonyl refining and resident mining on NEAs, then take the same blocks to the belt.

**A plausible belt arc** (stages, not dates):

1. **NEA retrievers** bring water and carbon to cislunar space (Keck-class bags).
2. **Resident NEA mines** stay put and ship containers on tugs.
3. **A Ceres hub:** water, ammonia and a shallow well (0.51 km/s escape) make it the belt's port and depot (tension 5 in [`studies/solar-resources/07-gamification-notes.md`](../studies/solar-resources/07-gamification-notes.md)).
4. **Metal bodies** supply belt yards with structure.
5. **The Mars–belt triangle trade:** Earth sells high tech to Mars, Mars sells bulk and low-tech goods to the belt, the belt sells volatiles and metal inward.

**What gates it:**

- **Power.** Sunlight at Ceres is about 180 W/m², an eighth of Earth's. Belt industry runs on reactors or very large mirrors, so **the belt depends on the inner system for fissile fuel** until fusion. No asteroid class is a meaningful uranium or thorium source.
- **Drives.** Chemical belt freight loses most of its cargo; MW-class electric tugs and nuclear thermal make the belt pay.
- **Windows and hubs.** Belt neighbours are about 5 km/s and decades of synodic time apart (Ceres–Vesta once every 17 years), so belt trade runs through hubs, not direct hops.

## Jupiter: what draws people past the belt

The Jovian system is water-rich, hostile and deep. Its resources aren't needed while the belt is young, so its pulls are mostly not about resources.

| Driver | What it is | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Prestige science** | Europa's ocean, estimated at about twice Earth's, under 10–30 km of ice; the search for life | Hosts again: **a new frontier starts a new contract era**, and the early-economy loop repeats at 5 AU |
| **The gateway to Saturn and beyond** | Saturn's rings (near-pure ice), Titan (the solar system's carbon and nitrogen capital), Enceladus (phosphate), Uranus (helium-3) | Mars terraforming needs about 1.9×10¹⁸ kg of nitrogen buffer gas, which Titan can supply; D-³He fusion needs helium-3 |
| **A base and waystation** | Callisto: NASA's HOPE study (2003) chose it for a surface base producing rocket propellant, a waystation for gravity-assist flybys, and remote exploration of Europa | Whoever runs outer-system traffic |
| **The Trojans** | Ice-and-organics bodies at Jupiter's L4 and L5, outside the radiation belts; with the Hildas and NEAs, about 2.6×10²⁰ kg | The next generation of prospectors: open claims, as the belt once was |
| **Fusion-era fuel** | Deuterium from any water; ocean-scale water on the Galilean moons and Trojans | Fusion fleets, once fusion drives exist |
| **Strategic depth** | Years from Earth with fission drives | Polities that want distance; it lasts until torch drives shrink the system |
| **The magnetosphere** | Electrodynamic tethers give propellant-free orbit changes inside the Jovian system | A niche: power is small (about 1 kW from a 20 km tether) |
| **People** | Once Callisto can support itself, people choose to live there | Settlers (next section) |

**What gates it:**

- **Radiation.** Callisto's surface dose is about 0.1 mSv a day, about 37 mSv a year *(calc)*: just over ten times Earth's background, but less than in low Earth orbit or on Mars. Europa's charged-particle flux is about 300 times Callisto's. **Callisto is the only comfortable foothold;** Europa is worked remotely or by machines.
- **Power.** Sunlight is about 50 W/m², so everything runs on reactors. HOPE's Callisto base used Brayton reactors of about 400 kWe in total.
- **Transit.** A Hohmann transfer from Earth takes 2.7 years. HOPE found 2–5-year round trips achievable only with significant propulsion advances (nuclear-electric, or fusion for under two years).
- **Metals and fissiles come from the inner system,** which makes Jupiter a customer of the belt and the Moon.

**Jupiter's real role:** a hub on the way to Saturn and the ice giants, and a strategic redoubt. The Saturn system is where the outer system's economy lives (rings, Titan, Enceladus), and Jupiter is the waystation and gravity assist that leads there.

## When the capability is there: people choosing where to live

Once a place can support itself and its burden per person has fallen, the reasons people move are the old ones. They become the main driver of late expansion.

| Pull | What it means here | Historical shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Room** | Habitat volume, land under a roof, a whole asteroid to hollow | Homesteads |
| **Law** | A place with different rules: looser, stricter, or one's own | Refuges and free ports |
| **Ideology and faith** | Communities that want to live apart | Massachusetts Bay, 1630 |
| **Family and birthplace** | The locally born have no Earth home; their children's home is wherever they were born | Every second generation |
| **Wages and cost of living** | Jobs where the multiplier is high and burden is low | Every boomtown |
| **Adventure and status** | Being first somewhere | Every frontier |

**In the model:** migration already reads wages against burden per person and risk ([`design/bases-settlements-and-the-civilian-economy.md`](bases-settlements-and-the-civilian-economy.md)). Add **affinities** per constituency: for distance from Earth, for a law regime, for an ideology, for a place's gravity. Then a free colony at Ceres draws people who would never move for wages alone.

## Independence from Earth: the capability ladder

What a place has to be able to do before Earth can no longer starve it. These are labels on regions of numbers the model already holds (closure by tier, stockpiles, population, research capacity), never states in code.

| Rung | Can… | Needs | Real anchor | Roughly where (bases doc labels) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0. Supplied** | Nothing alone | Everything imported | Every base today | Outpost |
| **1. Survive** | Keep a fixed population alive indefinitely under embargo | Closed air, water and food; tier 1; CHNOPS sources; enough people to do the work | ISS water recycling reaches about 98 %; oxygen recovery from CO₂ about 50 %, the rest needs local water. Salotti (2020) estimates about 110 people to do the survival work on Mars, without computers or robots | Company town |
| **2. Maintain** | Replace what wears out: life support, power, habitats, vehicles | Tiers 2–4; coarse tier-5 controllers; vitamin stockpiles measured in years | Metzger's crude early hardware at 2.5× the mass of Earth-built | Concession |
| **3. Grow** | Build new plant, fabs included; grow without imports | Tier 5 at a coarse node at least; a power source that doesn't need Earth's fuel | The growth law with v → 0 | Colony |
| **4. Innovate** | Improve its own blocks: research, engineering, training | Universities, labs, enough people for specialists | Without it, a polity is frozen at the last technology it bought | Colony to civilization |
| **5. Defend** | Build drives, warships, sensors, large optics; make fissile or fusion fuel | Tier 6; focal-plane arrays; enrichment or breeding, or fusion | The drive rights and export controls of the charter | Civilization |

**Cross-cutting needs** that every rung above 1 assumes:

- **Medicine:** surgeons, drugs, and the pharmaceutical chemistry behind them.
- **Education:** training the next generation locally, or importing every specialist.
- **Genetics:** a closed population needs enough people. Marin and Beluffi (2018) found about 98 people the minimum for a multi-generational ship under managed breeding; at 32, every simulation failed. Migration makes this irrelevant for any settlement still taking newcomers.
- **Governance and money:** courts, police, a currency people trust. Company scrip is the cheap first version.
- **Gravity and radiation health,** below.

### What each region is missing

| Region | Has | Missing before fusion | Independence path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Moon** | Oxygen, metals, polar water, **thorium and uranium feedstock** (KREEP), phosphorus | Carbon and nitrogen in quantity | Close the fuel cycle with lunar thorium; import C and N from NEAs |
| **Mars** | Most things: water, CO₂, nitrogen and argon in the air, iron; possible ores | Fissile processing; high-grade ores unproven | The most complete single body after Earth |
| **Belt** | Metals, water, CHNOPS at Ceres and C-types | **Fissiles**; strong sunlight | Fissile from the Moon or Mars, or fusion |
| **Jupiter** | Water, deuterium | Metals, fissiles | A customer of the belt and the inner system |
| **Saturn** | Water, carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus | Metals, fissiles | The same, unless a shake-up (a metal-cored Mimas) changes it |

**Independence before fusion is a property of a network.** No region outside Earth closes everything alone, so a belt–Mars–Moon network may be independent while none of its nodes is. This is the specialised-network bootstrapping strategy of the manufacturing doc, seen politically: **the lanes between the nodes are what an enemy cuts,** and the inner–outer trade axis is where the wars are.

**A shake-up can change it sooner.** The optional fissile-rich asteroid ([`studies/solar-resources/07-gamification-notes.md`](../studies/solar-resources/07-gamification-notes.md)) gives one region its own fuel feedstock before fusion. Its controller still has to build enrichment or breeding plant, which is the proliferation flashpoint moved outward, but the region's network can then climb to rung 3 and rung 5 without the inner system. Whoever holds it becomes a regional powerhouse.

**Fusion changes the map.** With catalysed D-D fusion, which needs nothing but water ([`design/resource-fields-grades-and-yield-curves.md`](resource-fields-grades-and-yield-curves.md), "Drives set the demand"), the belt and the outer system stop needing inner-system fissiles. That makes catalysed D-D the single biggest independence technology in the game, and a reason for Earth to control fusion know-how as tightly as drive rights.

### Gravity and radiation health

How much gravity people need for long-term health, and for having children, is unknown. That makes it a campaign variable in the same spirit as the resource priors.

| Body | Surface gravity *(calc)* |
| --- | --- |
| Mars | 0.38 g |
| Moon | 0.17 g |
| Ganymede | 0.15 g |
| Callisto | 0.13 g |
| Ceres | 0.029 g |
| Vesta | 0.026 g |

- **Proposal:** a drawn prior for the health cost of partial gravity, from "a sixth of a g is enough" to "anything under about a third of a g harms health over years". It sets which bodies can host locally born populations without spin habitats.
- **Spin habitats are the fallback,** at a cost in structure and burden per person. Ceres-born people living in spun habitats under Ceres's ice are a plausible end state either way.
- **Radiation** is already a field in the bases doc (dose by column and overburden). Callisto's surface is liveable with shielding; Europa's is not.

### How the ladder feeds the leverage curve

The dependency term of host leverage ([`design/intercept-window-initial-design-notes.md`](intercept-window-initial-design-notes.md)) reads the ladder directly:

- **Below rung 1,** defiance is suicide: Earth doesn't need a fleet.
- **At rung 2 with defences,** a break is survivable for as long as the stockpiles last.
- **At rung 3,** a break is durable: an embargo slows growth but doesn't stop it.
- **At rung 4,** the polity keeps up with Earth's technology instead of falling behind it.
- **At rung 5,** it can strike back.

The map shows the rung per settlement and per network, with uncertainty for anyone else's.

## Model proposal for Intercept Window

Almost everything here falls out of systems that already exist; the new parts are small.

- **Rings view:** a map layer showing the cheapest supply region for each good at each market, computed from the price field, yield curves and drive blocks. No new simulation.
- **A life-support basket per person:** kilograms a year of each CHNOPS element, water and oxygen, with recycling rates as engineered numbers (water near 98 %, oxygen from CO₂ near 50 % at the start). Population growth turns into N, C and P demand, which is what pulls the economy to C-types and Ceres.
- **Construction demand:** yards and habitats order structure by the tonne, which is what pulls it to metal asteroids.
- **Frontier milestones:** host budgets get a motive term per frontier (first Jovian landing, the Europa life search), so the contract era repeats at each new frontier.
- **Migration affinities:** per constituency, for distance from Earth, law, ideology and gravity.
- **The capability ladder:** computed per settlement and per network from closure by tier, stockpiles, population and research capacity; shown on the map and read by the leverage estimate.
- **A gravity-health prior,** drawn per campaign.
- **The world-climbs bench gains checks:** a belt hub appears in nearly every seed once nuclear drives exist, and a Jovian presence once fusion or MW-class nuclear-electric drives exist.

## Open questions

- [ ] The life-support basket: kilograms per person per year of each element, and recycling ceilings by tech.
- [ ] Gravity-health prior: its range, whether it's public at the start, and how it's discovered (births on the Moon are the experiment).
- [ ] Network independence: how the ladder is computed for a network rather than a node, and what counts as a network.
- [ ] Frontier milestones: which ones each host values, and whether they can be authored per scenario.
- [ ] Fusion know-how as a controlled good: a charter clause like drive rights?
- [ ] Is research capacity (rung 4) a stock of people and labs, a block, or both?
- [ ] Saturn deserves its own pass: rings, Titan, Enceladus and the nitrogen trade to Mars.
