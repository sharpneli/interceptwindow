# 04 — Outer System: Giant Planets, Moons, Rings, Pluto

Units: t = metric tonne. Confidence codes A–D as in `01-model.md`.

The outer system is where the **volatiles** are: water by the ocean-load, nitrogen, methane, ammonia, deuterium, helium-3. It is metal-poor and fissile-poor (rocky cores are chondritic and buried under ice).

---

## Giant planet atmospheres (fusion fuels and hydrogen)

| Planet | H₂ / He (by volume) | ³He/⁴He | D/H (in H₂) | Low-orbit speed (km/s) | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jupiter | ~89.8 % / ~10.2 % | ~1–1.7×10⁻⁴ (Galileo probe) | ~2×10⁻⁵ (≈ protosolar) | ~42 | A |
| Saturn | ~96 % / ~3–4 % (He uncertain) | assumed protosolar-like | ~2×10⁻⁵ | ~25 | B |
| Uranus | ~82.5 % / ~15 % (+ ~2 % CH₄) | — | higher than Jupiter/Saturn, lower than Earth's oceans | ~15 | B/C |
| Neptune | ~80 % / ~19 % (+ CH₄) | — | as Uranus | ~16.6 | B/C |

**³He grade (atmospheric mining studies):** NASA's outer-planet atmospheric mining studies assume a ³He mass fraction of **~1.5×10⁻⁵ for Uranus** and **~1.9×10⁻⁵ for Neptune** → ~1 t of ³He per ~50 000–70 000 t of gas processed. Compare the Moon at 20 ppb: ~1 t per 50 million t of regolith. **Ice giant atmospheres are ~1 000× richer ore** than lunar soil, with the hydrogen by-product usable as propellant.

**Why Uranus is the classic ³He target:** highest He fraction, shallowest well of the four giants (escape 21.3 km/s; low orbit ~15 km/s), and a cold, methane-poor upper atmosphere. Jupiter is the worst: deepest well and lethal radiation belts. Saturn is in the middle and has a benign environment.

**Model note:** gas-giant ³He only makes sense with nuclear propulsion (nuclear thermal at minimum, realistically fusion/pulse). Treat it as a **late-game, drive-tech-gated resource**. NASA studies sized mining "cruisers" around gas-core nuclear rockets bringing back ~500 kg of ³He per trip.

## Jupiter system

| Body | Resources | Environment | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Io** | Sulfur, SO₂, silicate volcanism; Na, K, Cl salts | Extreme radiation; deepest in the well | A/B |
| **Europa** | Water: global ocean estimated at **~2× Earth's oceans**, under ~10–30 km of ice; surface salts | Very high radiation | B/C |
| **Ganymede** | Water: largest ocean in the solar system (liquid ~6–8× Earth's; total water incl. ice perhaps tens of times Earth's), ~100 km ocean under ~150 km of ice; has its own magnetic field | Moderate radiation | C |
| **Callisto** | Water ice + rock, ocean beneath ~135–150 km of ice; ancient dirty surface | **Lowest radiation of the Galilean moons** → best Jovian base | B/C |
| Small inner moons / ring | Dust, rock | High radiation | B |
| Magnetosphere | Strongest in the system; antiproton trapping expected but Saturn may be the better natural trap (see `05`) | — | C |

**Model note:** Jupiter's system is **water-rich but hostile and deep**. Callisto is the only comfortable foothold. Jovian moon oceans are under kilometres of ice — inaccessible as bulk resources; the *surface ice* is what counts.

## Saturn system — the outer system's natural hub

### Rings

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | **1.54 ± 0.49×10¹⁹ kg** (~0.4 Mimas; ~0.02 % of the Moon) | A |
| Composition | **>99 % water ice** (<~1 % "dirt") | A |
| D/H | Close to Earth's oceans (VSMOW) | B |
| Age | Likely 10–100 Myr | B |
| Loss rate ("ring rain") | ~430–2 900 kg/s into Saturn → drained in ~300 Myr | B |

1.5×10¹⁹ kg of near-pure ice is ~1 % of Earth's ocean mass, pre-crushed into particles, in orbit. Δv inside the system is the main cost. **The cleanest, most pre-processed water in the solar system.**

### Titan

| Resource | Amount | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquid hydrocarbons in lakes and seas** | ~9 000 km³ (≈ 2 000 mi³), mostly methane-dominated with ethane; ≈ 40× Earth's proven oil reserves by volume; ~35× Earth's fossil fuel reserves and ~300× proven natural gas reserves by mass | B |
| Distribution | Nearly all in a 900×1 800 km northern box; Kraken Mare holds ~80 % of surface liquids; Ligeia Mare ~170–200 m deep | B |
| Atmospheric hydrocarbons | ~7× the lake inventory | B |
| Dune organics (equatorial) | More than Earth's coal reserves | C |
| **Nitrogen** | Thick N₂ atmosphere (~1.5 bar surface pressure, standard value) | A |
| Water | Ice crust ("bedrock") + subsurface ocean | B |

**Model note:** Titan is **the carbon and nitrogen capital of the solar system** in an accessible form, with a thick atmosphere that makes landing cheap and aerobraking possible. Low gravity (escape 2.6 km/s) but the dense atmosphere penalizes ascent. Hydrocarbons are a *chemical feedstock* (plastics, CH₄ propellant), not an energy source by themselves — there's no free oxygen to burn them with.

### Enceladus

| Resource | Notes | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Subsurface salty ocean, vented through south-polar plumes | Sampled by Cassini in the plume and the E-ring | A |
| **Phosphate** | Orthophosphates at **≥100× Earth's ocean concentration** | A/B |
| Salts | Na, K, Cl, carbonates; moderately alkaline ocean | A |
| H₂ (hydrothermal) | Indicates water–rock reactions at the seafloor | A |
| Organics | Rich variety | A |

**Model note:** Enceladus is a **free, self-delivering sample of an ocean** — its plumes launch material into orbit. The phosphorus finding is significant: P is a classic bottleneck for agriculture/life support off Earth.

### Other Saturnian moons

Rhea, Dione, Tethys, Iapetus: water-ice dominated, low gravity. Phoebe: captured outer-system object with the **highest D/H ratio yet measured** in the solar system and unusual ¹³C/¹²C — a curiosity, and a deuterium-rich ice source.

### Saturn's magnetosphere

Bickford's NASA-funded study suggested Saturn's ring–cosmic-ray interactions make it the **best natural antiproton trap** in the solar system, better than Earth or Jupiter. Quantities are still minuscule (see `05-fissiles-and-exotics.md`). Conf. C/D.

## Uranus & Neptune systems

| Body | Resources | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Uranus atmosphere | ³He (see above), H₂, CH₄ | B/C |
| Uranian moons (Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, Oberon) | Water ice + rock; possible ammonia; possible subsurface oceans in larger ones | C |
| Neptune atmosphere | ³He, H₂, CH₄ | B/C |
| **Triton** (captured Kuiper belt object) | **N₂ ice**, CH₄, CO, water ice; active nitrogen geysers. Upper limit on surface N₂: ~0.5–1 km global average thickness | B/C |

Long transit times (decades with chemical propulsion) dominate cost far more than Δv.

## Pluto (and large TNOs)

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Sputnik Planitia | ~1 000 km-wide basin filled with a ~1–2 km thick sheet of N₂ ice with CH₄ and CO | B |
| N₂ inventory (surface, mostly Sputnik Planitia) | ~(0.4–3)×10²⁰ mol ≈ **1–8×10¹⁸ kg N₂** (derived) — comparable to Earth's entire atmospheric N₂ | B/C |

**Model note:** Pluto alone holds an Earth-atmosphere's worth of nitrogen as solid ice. Irrelevant to near-term economics, but a striking fact for a far-future frontier.

## Summary: the outer system as a resource region

| Need | Best outer-system source | Runner-up |
|---|---|---|
| Water (pure) | Saturn's rings | Callisto surface, Saturnian icy moons |
| Carbon (accessible) | Titan seas & atmosphere | Trojans, comets |
| Nitrogen | Titan atmosphere | Triton, Pluto |
| Phosphorus | Enceladus plumes | (C-type asteroids in the inner system) |
| ³He | Uranus atmosphere | Saturn atmosphere |
| Deuterium-enriched water | Phoebe, comets | Enceladus |
| Metals, fissiles | — essentially none accessible — | import from the inner system |

**This is the core structural tension of the whole map:** the inner system has metals and fissiles but is volatile-poor; the outer system has volatiles but no metals or fissiles. Trade (or conflict) along that axis is built in.
