# 02 — Inner System: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Moon, Mars, Phobos & Deimos

Units: t = metric tonne; ppm/ppb by mass unless noted. Confidence codes A–D as defined in `01-model.md`.

---

## Mercury

| Resource | Where | Amount / grade | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water ice | Permanently shadowed polar craters (both poles) | ~10¹⁴–10¹⁵ kg total (≈100–1 000 km³); upper limit ~3×10¹⁵ kg. Buried layer is near-pure ice under 10–30 cm of hydrogen-poorer cover | B |
| Radioactives | Surface average (N. hemisphere) | K 1 150 ppm, **Th 0.22 ppm, U 0.09 ppm** | B |
| Sulfur | Crust | ≥10× Earth/Moon silicate levels | B |
| Iron | Core is huge, surface Fe is low | Highly reduced surface | B |

**Model notes**
- Mercury holds **more polar ice than the Moon** (roughly 100–1 000× on current estimates), and it is cleaner and thicker. The catch is position: deep in the Sun's well, extreme thermal cycling, and ~4.3 km/s surface escape.
- Fissile-poor. Th at 0.22 ppm is ~3× below Mars and ~50× below Earth's continental crust.
- Unlimited solar power. A natural home for energy-intensive industry (beamed power, mass drivers) if the setting wants one.

## Venus

| Resource | Where | Amount / grade | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO₂ | Atmosphere (~4.8×10²⁰ kg total, standard value) | ~96.5 % | A |
| **Nitrogen (N₂)** | Atmosphere, ~3.5 % | ≈1.7×10¹⁹ kg — **~4× Earth's atmospheric N₂** (derived from standard values) | A |
| Sulfuric acid / SO₂ | Cloud decks (~48–70 km) | Abundant | A |
| U / Th / K (Vega 1) | Lowland basalt | U 0.64 ppm, Th 1.5 ppm, K 0.45 % | A (low precision) |
| U / Th / K (Vega 2) | Lowland basalt | U 0.68 ppm, Th 2.0 ppm, K 0.40 % | A (low precision) |
| U / Th / K (Venera 8) | Anomalous site | **Th 6.5 ppm, U 2.2 ppm, K₂O ~4 %** — granitoid-like | A (low precision) |

**Model notes**
- Venus is the **largest accessible nitrogen reservoir in the inner system** and a carbon reservoir, reachable from cloud-level aerostats without touching the surface.
- The Venera 8 result implies evolved, possibly granitic crust exists → Venus could in principle host Th/U-enriched terrains comparable to Earth's continents. Practically inaccessible (470 °C, 90 bar). Treat as a long-term, high-tech reserve.
- Highland tesserae and Ishtar Terra have never been sampled → confidence D for anything there.

## Earth (baseline for comparison)

Earth is the only body with **ore-grade** deposits of almost everything, because of water + plate tectonics + biology (Rule 3 in `01-model.md`).

| Resource | Amount | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Water | ~1.335×10⁹ km³ total (≈1.4×10²¹ kg) | A |
| Uranium, identified recoverable (< USD 260/kgU, 1 Jan 2023) | **~7.93 Mt U** (60 % reasonably assured, 40 % inferred) | A |
| …of which < USD 130/kgU | ~5.9 Mt U | A |
| Uranium, undiscovered (prognosticated + speculative) | ~7.9 Mt U | C |
| Uranium, unconventional (e.g. phosphates) | ~57 Mt U | C |
| Uranium in seawater (~3.3 ppb, standard value) | ~4.5×10⁹ t U (derived) | A (very low grade) |
| Thorium (Australia alone, identified in situ, end 2024) | ~1.39 Mt Th | B |
| Crustal U background | ~1–4 ppm | A |
| Deuterium in oceans (D/H 1.56×10⁻⁴) | ~5×10¹³ t D (derived) | A |
| Helium-3 in atmosphere | ~37 000 t (at 7 ppt; not extractable cheaply) | A |
| Atmospheric N₂ (standard value) | ~3.9×10¹⁸ kg | A |

**Uranium geography on Earth** (identified recoverable): Australia 28 %, Kazakhstan 14 %, Canada 10 %. **A single deposit, Olympic Dam, holds ~17 % of the world's identified resources** (produced as a copper by-product). Niger, ~6 %, has seen disruption since 2023. → Terrestrial fissile supply is already geographically concentrated and politically exposed.

**Model note:** Earth's weakness is the 9.4 km/s ascent and atmosphere. Its strength is unmatched ore grades, industry and all the enrichment/reprocessing infrastructure. Exports from Earth should be "high value per kg" goods: fissile material, electronics, people, complex machinery.

## Moon

### Water and volatiles (south & north polar cold traps)

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Total PSR area | ~31 000 km² (13 361 km² north, 17 698 km² south) | B |
| Ice grade at Cabeus (LCROSS impact) | ~5.6 wt % in regolith | A |
| If all PSRs held 5.6 wt % in top 1 m | ~2.9×10⁹ t water | C (upper-ish scenario) |
| North pole radar (Mini-SAR) | > 600 Mt | B/C |
| Lunar Prospector neutrons, upper estimate | up to ~6×10⁹ t (both poles) | C |
| "10 m pure ice in all cold traps" scenario | ~10⁸ t | C |
| Richest individual craters (multi-dataset compilation) | Cabeus ~11 Mt, Shoemaker ~5 Mt, … | C |

**Baseline choice:** use **~1 Gt total lunar polar water**, with most of the *exploitable* fraction concentrated in a few dozen named south-polar craters (top crater ~10 Mt scale). Wide uncertainty is real: estimates span ~10⁸ to ~6×10⁹ t.

Other volatiles seen in the LCROSS plume: CO, H₂, NH₃, H₂S, CO₂, CH₄ (traces). Lunar polar ice is **not pure**; it's "dirty, multi-volatile ice", which actually makes it a modest N and C source.

### Regolith (everywhere)

| Resource | Where | Grade | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | All regolith (silicates/oxides) | ~40–45 wt % (bound) | A |
| Titanium, iron (ilmenite) | Nearside high-Ti maria (e.g. Tranquillitatis) | Ilmenite up to roughly 10–20 % in high-Ti mare basalts (less in soils) | B |
| Metallic iron (fine grains) | All mature regolith | ~0.1–1 % | A |
| **Helium-3** | Mature high-Ti maria soils retain most | **~10–20 ppb** in Ti-rich soils; lower elsewhere | A (Apollo samples) |
| He-3 total inventory | Whole-Moon regolith | > 1 Mt (estimate) | C |

He-3 correlates with Ti (ilmenite traps it) and with regolith maturity (exposure time). Mining requires processing ~50 000 t of regolith per kg of He-3 at 20 ppb (100 % recovery). Interlune targets the 2030s for harvesting and is flying a He-3 mapping camera on Astrolab's FLIP rover.

### KREEP: lunar thorium, uranium, rare earths

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Lunar surface Th range | ~0.1 to 12 ppm | B |
| Typical U / Th | U ~0.3 ppm average (up to ~2 ppm in the PKT), Th ~1–2 ppm average (up to ~7 ppm in the PKT); U/Th ≈ 0.27 | B |
| **Procellarum KREEP Terrane (PKT)** | Nearside, Oceanus Procellarum & Imbrium; ~16 % of lunar surface | B |
| **Compton–Belkovich** (farside silicic volcanic complex, ~25×35 km) | Th **14–26 ppm** (reconstruction), earlier ~40–55 ppm; Th spread up to 300 km east at ~2 ppm | B |
| Other silicic domes (all in PKT) | Hansteen Alpha, Mairan hills, Lassell Massif, Gruithuisen Domes | B |
| Lunar granite samples | Th 20–65 ppm | A |
| urKREEP total REE | ~1 200 ppm; Y, Ce ~300 ppm each; Nd ~180 ppm, Dy ~65 ppm | A/C |

**Worked estimate (derived, for scale):**
- PKT ≈ 6×10⁶ km². Top 10 m of regolith at 1.5 t/m³ ≈ 9×10¹³ t of soil. At ~4 ppm Th → **~3.6×10⁸ t Th**; at U/Th 0.27 → **~1×10⁸ t U**. That's >10× Earth's identified recoverable uranium — **but at ~1–7 ppm**, i.e. *the grade of ordinary continental granite*, which no terrestrial miner would touch.
- Compton–Belkovich: 1 km³ of felsic rock at ~20 ppm Th and 2.6 t/m³ → **~50 000 t Th per km³**. Small, but the richest known off-Earth thorium rock.

**Model note:** the Moon is the **only off-Earth bulk fissile feedstock in a shallow well**. The prize is the nearside PKT (large, low grade) and a handful of silicic domes (small, highest grade). Uranium-rich micro-minerals exist (e.g. yttrobetafite) but as grains, not ore bodies.

## Mars

### Water ice

| Reservoir | Volume | Global equivalent layer (GEL) | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| South polar layered deposits | ~1.6×10⁶ km³ | ~11 m | B |
| North polar layered deposits | similar order (~10⁶ km³) | — | B |
| Mid-latitude debris-covered glaciers (LDA, LVF, CCF, ~30–50° lat) | Deposit volume 4.16×10⁵ km³; ice 1.25–3.74×10⁵ km³ | 0.9–2.6 m | B/C |
| Glacier-like forms (1 243 mapped) | 523–1 570 km³ ice (480–1 440 Gt) | 3–10 mm | B |
| **Medusae Fossae Formation (equatorial!)** | Ice-rich unit under 300–600 m dry cover | ~1.5–2.7 m (30–50 % of NPLD) | C |
| Shallow ground ice (high latitudes) | Widespread in the upper meter | — | A/B |

**Model note:** Mars is water-rich but water is **mostly at the poles or buried at mid-latitudes**. The Medusae Fossae discovery is strategically interesting: a large equatorial ice reservoir (easier landings, warmer, more sunlight) but under hundreds of metres of overburden.

### Atmosphere

CO₂ ~95 %, N₂ ~2–3 %, Ar ~2 % (standard values). Thin (~0.6 % of Earth's surface pressure) but free for the taking: CO₂ → O₂ + CO/CH₄ (with water), and **argon** is a usable inert/propellant gas.

### Radioactives and metals

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| K | 2 000–6 000 ppm | B |
| **Th** | **0.2–1 ppm, average ~0.6 ppm** | B |
| Th variability | Much less than the Moon's (0.1–12 ppm) | B |
| Highest-Th region (mid-latitudes) | Northern Acidalia Planitia | B |
| Hematite concretions ("blueberries", Meridiani) | Iron ore at the surface | A |
| Hydrothermal minerals (smectite, silica, zeolite, serpentine, carbonate, chlorite) in impact craters | Evidence of past hydrothermal systems | B |

**Model note:** Mars is the **only off-Earth body where real ore bodies are geologically plausible** — volcanic provinces (Tharsis as a large igneous province analogue: Ni, Cu, PGM, Cr potential), impact hydrothermal systems (Earth's impact structures host U, Au, Cu, Ni ores), aqueous concretions. **None is confirmed.** Bulk Th grade is low; any fissile ore on Mars is confidence D. This is the most defensible place to "plant" a discovery.

Perchlorates in Martian soil are a hazard and an oxidizer/chlorine source.

## Phobos & Deimos

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Phobos ~22 km, Deimos ~13 km | A |
| Composition | Unknown: spectra resemble dark primitive asteroids; origin debated (captured asteroid vs. Mars impact ejecta) | C |
| Δv | Tiny from Mars transfer orbit (see `01-model.md`) | A |
| In the game | Origin is a campaign draw (MMX samples would settle it by 2031, after the game's knowledge cutoff); public in a 2030–2040 start | — |

**Model note:** Two hypotheses → two very different resource profiles:
- *Captured C-type:* hydrated minerals, organics, maybe ice at depth → **a fuel depot in Mars orbit.**
- *Mars ejecta:* dry, Mars-like basalt → mostly useful as radiation shielding and construction mass.

Each campaign draws one of the two (proposal: 50/50) and, for a 2030s start, tells everyone at setup.
