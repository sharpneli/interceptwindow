# 03 — Small Bodies: NEAs, Main Belt, Trojans, Comets, Kuiper Belt, Oort Cloud

Units: t = metric tonne. Confidence codes A–D as in `01-model.md`.

---

## Asteroid taxonomy → resource class (the cheat sheet)

| Spectral class | Meteorite analogue | What it's good for | Water | Metal | U/Th |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C-complex** (C, B, Ch, Cg…) | Carbonaceous chondrites (CI, CM) | Water, carbon, organics, N (ammonium), P, salts | **~5–20 wt %** (as hydrated minerals; more where ice survives) | Minor, oxidized | ~ppb (chondritic) |
| **S-complex** | Ordinary chondrites | Silicates, free Fe-Ni metal grains (several % to ~20 %), PGMs at chondritic levels | Dry | Moderate | ~ppb |
| **M / X (metal-rich)** | Iron meteorites, stony-irons, mesosiderites | Fe, Ni (~8 %), Co, **PGMs (~10 ppm), Au (~1 ppm)** | Dry | **High** | **≈ none** (lithophile elements went to the crust that was stripped) |
| **V-type** | HED basalts (from Vesta) | Basalt, some Ti/Cr; crustal lithophiles at modest levels | Dry | Low | Few tenths of ppm (order of magnitude) |
| **D / P** | Primitive outer-system material (Tagish Lake–like?) | Organics, ices at depth, carbon | Likely ice-rich | Low | ~ppb |

**Key point for fissiles:** no asteroid class is a meaningful U/Th source. Chondrites carry U at roughly 0.01 ppm and Th at roughly 0.03 ppm — roughly 300× below Earth's upper continental crust — and metal asteroids carry essentially none.

**Flagship metal NEA: (6178) 1986 DA**

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter | ~2.8–3.2 km | B |
| Surface | ~85 % metal (spectral fit); radar-bright | B |

**Model notes**
- NEAs are the **cheapest volatiles and metals for cislunar space**: several thousand known objects are energetically easier to return from than the lunar surface. Their drawback is *synodic timing*: launch windows to a given NEA may be years apart.
- Most NEAs are rubble piles (Bennu has >50 % porosity) → easy to dig, hard to anchor to.

## Key main-belt bodies

### Ceres — the belt's water/ammonia/salt capital

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter / density | ~940 km / 2 162 kg/m³ | A |
| Water : rock | ~47 : 53 by volume, ~25 : 75 by mass (if rock anhydrous) | B |
| Implied water | **~2.3×10²⁰ kg** — about a sixth of Earth's oceans (derived) | B/C |
| Crust | Ice-rich but <30–40 vol % ice; mixed with hydrated salts, clathrates, silicates; ~1 km dry regolith on top | B |
| Ammonium-bearing clays | Global — implies outer-solar-system ingredients | A/B |
| Sodium carbonates & salts | Widespread; bright faculae (Occator) are salt deposits | A/B |
| Deep brines | Likely persist at the base of the crust (eutectic ~220–245 K) | C |
| Organics | Global carbon component; local aliphatic organics | B |
| Escape velocity | 0.51 km/s | A |

**Model note:** Ceres combines a trivially shallow gravity well with water, **nitrogen (ammonia)**, carbon and salts. It is the natural capital, port and fuel depot of the belt. Its only weaknesses are the inclination-change cost to get there and a lack of fissiles and metals.

### Vesta — differentiated protoplanet

Basaltic crust (HED meteorites), olivine mantle exposed in the Rheasilvia basin, iron core. A source of basalt, Ti/Cr, and the **only belt body with a real crust** — so the best (still poor) asteroidal source of lithophile elements like Th/U/REE. Escape velocity ~0.36 km/s.

### 16 Psyche — the largest metal-rich asteroid

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Share of belt mass | ~1 % | B |
| Bulk density | ~3.4–4.1 g/cm³ (estimates: 3.78 ± 0.34; 3.88; 3.99 ± 0.26) | B |
| Metal fraction | Up to ~60 vol % (assuming ≤20 % porosity); one model: 82.5 % metal + 7 % low-Fe pyroxene + 10.5 % carbonaceous chondrite at ~35 % porosity | C |
| Interpretation | Remnant core with some mantle, or a rubble pile of metal + silicate; *not* the solid ~95 % iron ball once assumed | C |
| In the game | Metal fraction is a campaign draw (proposal: uniform 30–80 % by mass); the real orbiter arrives Aug 2029, so a 2030s start makes the draw public | — |

**Model note:** even at the pessimistic end, Psyche holds ~10¹⁶ t of Fe-Ni — millions of years of current terrestrial steel output. The **headline "quintillion-dollar" valuations are meaningless** (prices would collapse), but the strategic value as a structural-metal and PGM source for in-space construction is real. Its metal fraction is drawn per campaign, so the belt's metal economy differs from game to game.

**Other notable M/X bodies:** (22) Kalliope — 40–60 % metal, silicate-rich surface ("mini-Mercury"). Many smaller M-types in the middle belt.

### Ice-rich large asteroids

Hygiea and (704) Interamnia (density ~2.0 g/cm³) are, like Ceres, water-rich. Secondary water hubs in the outer belt.

## Returned-sample ground truth (C-types)

### Bennu (OSIRIS-REx, returned 2023)

| Finding | Significance |
|---|---|
| Abundant water-bearing clays | Water extractable by heating |
| Evaporite salt sequence: Na phosphates, Na carbonates, sulfates, chlorides, fluorides, halite, sylvite (11 brine minerals) | **Phosphorus** and salts accessible; parent body had liquid brines |
| **Ammonia ~75× Ryugu's**, more than most carbonaceous chondrites | **Nitrogen** source; parent body formed beyond Jupiter |
| Amino acids (14 of 20 protein-forming), all 5 nucleobases | Organic feedstock |
| Phosphorus-rich, boron-poor brine chemistry | Different from Earth's |

### Ryugu (Hayabusa2)

CI-like, hydrated, carbonates and phosphates; less ammonia than Bennu.

**Model note:** C-type NEAs are effectively **"chemical feedstock asteroids"** — water + C + N + P + salts. That combination (CHNOPS) is what life support and agriculture need, and it's the scarcest bundle in the inner system outside Earth.

## Comets

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Bulk density | ~500 kg/m³ (very porous) | A |
| Refractory : ice (67P) | ~1:1 to ~3:1 by mass; compositional constraint 0.5–1.7 | B |
| CO relative to water (67P) | 2.7–20 % | A |
| CO₂ relative to water (67P, near-surface) | ~30 % (molar) | B |
| NH₃ | Nitrogen-depleted relative to solar; NH₃/H₂O ~1.5 % in Halley, ~10× lower in 67P | A |
| **D/H in water** | 67P: **5.3×10⁻⁴ (3.4× Earth's oceans)**; Oort cloud comets ~2× Earth; 103P/Hartley 2 ≈ Earth | A |
| Refractory part | ~1:1 organics : minerals | A |

**Model note:** comets are **volatile bombs** (water, CO, CO₂, organics) and the **richest natural deuterium source per kg of water**. They're also fragile and outgas near the Sun. A Jupiter-family comet is a seasonal resource.

## Oort cloud

| Item | Value | Conf. |
|---|---|---|
| Number of comets | ~10¹² (order of magnitude) | D |
| Outer cloud mass | ~5 Earth masses if Halley is typical (older estimates up to 380 M⊕) | D |
| Inner (Hills) cloud | Essentially unconstrained — dominates uncertainty in the solar system's total mass budget | D |

**Model note:** the largest volatile reservoir in the system by far, and functionally irrelevant without interstellar-class propulsion. Good as an "end-game frontier" or a source of rogue long-period comets passing through the inner system (a temporary, contested resource).
